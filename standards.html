The library

What each standard proves — and who it is for

Certifiable2022

ISO/IEC 27001

The information security management system standard. Audited by a certification body, it results in a certificate that procurement, investors and enterprise customers recognise worldwide.

  • Clauses 4–10 + 93 Annex A controls
  • Stage 1 / Stage 2 audit model
  • Surveillance yearly, re-certification every 3 years
Most requested
Certifiable2023

ISO/IEC 42001 (AIMS)

AI management system. For teams building or operating AI systems who need a defensible answer on governance, data, lifecycle and impact — and a route into EU AI Act alignment.

  • AI inventory and risk classification
  • Data governance and lifecycle control
  • Built on the Annex SL structure, like 27001
AI teams
Extension2019

ISO/IEC 27701

Privacy information management. Extends ISO 27001 and 27701 controls to GDPR-style accountability — the cleanest way to demonstrate that privacy is managed, not merely documented.

  • Records of processing
  • Controller and processor controls
  • Adds directly onto an existing ISMS
Privacy
Certifiable2019

ISO/IEC 20000-1

IT service management. For companies whose service delivery itself is the product and whose customers ask how incidents, changes and continuity are managed.

  • Service level and incident management
  • Change and release control
  • Frequent in public tenders
IT service providers
ReportAICPA

SOC 2

An auditor's report, not a certificate — the de facto entry ticket for SaaS selling into North America. Type I tests design; Type II tests operating effectiveness over a period.

  • Security, availability, confidentiality
  • Processing integrity and privacy optional
  • Overlaps heavily with ISO 27001 controls
US market
FrameworkNIST

NIST CSF 2.0

The common language for cyber risk: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond, Recover. Not certifiable — but excellent as the structure underneath your programme and board reporting.

  • Six functions, outcome-based
  • Profile and tier self-assessment
  • Maps cleanly to ISO 27001
Risk language
DirectiveEU

NIS2

Cybersecurity obligations for essential and important entities across sectors. Governance, risk measures, incident reporting and supply chain security — expected to be demonstrable, not aspirational.

  • Management accountability
  • Incident reporting timelines
  • ISO 27001 is the natural evidence base
Regulated sectors
RegulationEU

EU AI Act

Risk-based rules for AI systems placed on the EU market, phasing in through 2026–2027. Prohibited practices, transparency duties and obligations that scale with the system's risk class.

  • AI system inventory first
  • Classification drives the controls
  • ISO/IEC 42001 as the operating framework
AI in production
RegulationEU

GDPR

Data protection accountability: lawful basis, records, data subject rights, transfers and breach handling. Continuous, not one-off — supported by ISO 27701 for structure.

  • Records of processing activities
  • DPIA triggers and vendor agreements
  • Privacy by design in the SDLC
Everyone
